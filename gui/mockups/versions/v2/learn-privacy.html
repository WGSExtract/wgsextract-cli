Privacy & sharing

A genome file uniquely identifies you and carries half of each parent’s and child’s DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) alongside your own.

Analysis runs on the computer holding your files

WGS Extract processes BAM (Binary Alignment Map), CRAM (Compressed Reference-oriented Alignment Map) and VCF (Variant Call Format) files directly on the machine hosting its local server; phone access over Wi-Fi acts only as a remote screen, while pointing at another server trusts that machine with your files.

This clickable mockup uses fictional genomes and simulated runs. Because its review bar saves notes to a shared database, never paste real genotypes or medical details into review notes.

Fetching public reference files versus sending sample data

Downloading public references, ClinVar releases or containers (such as the 0.9 GB hs38d1 reference for Alex’s BAM) reveals ordinary web connection details like your IP (Internet Protocol) address while sending zero letters of sample DNA.

Default analyses never upload sample positions or genotypes; any remote lookup sending sample data requires an explicit opt-in naming what goes where. External reference links open public sites in a new tab, while Learn links stay local.

Sharing the smallest slice that answers the question

  • Share only what is needed: a single-gene region extract or small variant table exposes far less than a 90 GB BAM, though any slice stays sensitive.
  • Stripping your name never anonymizes DNA: internal headers keep sample tags, and a few dozen markers identify people via relative databases.
  • Before uploading a kit to a matching site, read its rules on law-enforcement queries, research opt-ins, retention and backup deletion.
  • Store backups on encrypted drives, use access-controlled transfers and never post sample slices on public issue trackers.

Get clear consent before analyzing or uploading a relative’s file: because Gordon, Helen and Reginald share half their autosomal DNA plus direct lineage markers, one upload exposes family members who never chose to test.
