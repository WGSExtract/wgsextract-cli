Your file journey

Reads are evidence. Calls are inferences. Reports add interpretation. Keep the evidence if you can.

FASTQ: the original read sequences

FASTQ means FASTA with Quality scores. Each record contains a read name, sequence and quality score for each base. Short paired-end data usually arrives as two compressed files, R1 and R2, with corresponding records.

Compressed 30× short-read human data may take roughly 30–100 GB. Size depends on read length, quality encoding and compression. FASTQ has no reference coordinates.

Align: place the reads on a reference

Alignment searches for likely placements of reads against a reference. bwa, bwa-mem2 and bwa-mem3 are short-read options; minimap2 is commonly used for long reads. A reference-specific aligner index speeds the search.

Whole-genome alignment may take hours and substantial working disk. Repeated regions can remain ambiguous even when the program finishes successfully.

BAM & CRAM: reads with their placements

BAM means Binary Alignment Map. It stores read sequences, quality scores, placements and other alignment fields. A 30× short-read human BAM is often around 60–100 GB.

CRAM means Compressed Reference-oriented Alignment Map. It compresses the same kind of records more tightly, often to tens of GB. In normal reference-based use it needs the exact reference sequence to decode the reads.

CRAM can preserve records losslessly, but some labs use quality-score reduction. Conversion cannot restore detail already discarded. Keep an original until a new copy is verified and backed up.

Call: infer genotypes from evidence

Variant callers infer which alleles are present. bcftools, GATK and DeepVariant use different methods. Choose a caller and model for your sequencing technology and the region’s ploidy.

A small-variant caller does not comprehensively test structural changes, repeat expansions or every complex gene. A finished VCF is not a guarantee of correct calls.

VCF & gVCF: calls, not reads

VCF means Variant Call Format. A typical compressed whole-genome small-variant VCF takes hundreds of MB. It records coordinates, alleles, genotypes and quality fields, but cannot recreate the reads.

gVCF means genomic VCF. It adds records or blocks for assessed non-variant regions and their evidence. This helps joint genotyping and reference-aware interpretation, but block quality and caller compatibility still matter.

A site missing from an ordinary variant-only VCF is unknown, not automatically 0/0. It may be unsequenced, filtered or confidently reference. See a concrete example.

Indexes are the table of contents

An index lets software jump to a region without scanning an entire file. BAM commonly uses .bai or .csi, CRAM .crai, compressed VCF .tbi or .csi, and FASTA .fai.

Keep indexes alongside their files. Rebuild them after changing the data. An aligner’s large reference index is a different object, not a substitute for a BAM index.

What should I keep?

  • Original evidence: keep your lab’s FASTQ and/or read-preserving BAM/CRAM if feasible, plus backups. Ask what processing or quality reduction the lab applied.
  • Exact reference: keep the FASTA identity and checksum, especially for CRAM. A human FASTA is about 3 GB uncompressed; aligner indexes can be much larger.
  • Results and provenance: keep VCF/gVCF, indexes, tool versions, settings and the result manifest. Reports alone are hard to reanalyze.

These sizes are planning examples, not promises. Long-read runs, extra depth and temporary sort files change storage needs significantly.

DNA-test exports are a smaller side route

A DNA-test file contains genotypes for a selected microarray marker list. WGS Extract can create a company-style table from usable sequencing calls. It does not turn a kit into whole-genome reads.

Missing markers stay uncertain, formats and site policies change, and a receiving service may reject a generated file. Uploading shares genetic data: review privacy first.

Programs need the right computer and dependencies

The CPU runs general work; RAM holds working data; disk holds files. A GPU accelerates only software built for it. NVIDIA CUDA and Apple Metal are different platforms.

Docker packages programs in containers. A Pixi environment groups software packages. WGS Extract checks prerequisites; missing software needs separate installation. Availability and timings depend on the machine, not just the input size.
