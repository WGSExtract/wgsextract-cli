A kit and a genome: what’s the difference?

A DNA-testing company doesn’t read the whole genome. Its chip reads a fixed set of about 600,000–700,000 markers, around 0.02% of the genome’s 3 billion letters, chosen because they differ between people. That’s plenty for finding relatives, and a rough start on lineage and ancestry.

A whole genome reads nearly every letter, about 30 times over. It gives exact haplogroups and narrower ancestry intervals, and it’s what variant calling, health-related variants and Y-STR markers need.
