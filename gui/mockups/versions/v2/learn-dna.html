DNA & genomes

Your genome is a sequence of molecules, not a prediction of your future.

Four letters, paired into a long molecule

DNA means deoxyribonucleic acid. Its information is stored in the order of four bases, written A, C, G and T. Picture a twisted ladder: each side is a strand, with A paired to T and C paired to G across the rungs.

A base pair (bp) is one rung. A sequence of 150 bp describes 150 positions, not 300 independent instructions. A digital genome file records estimates of those letters, not the physical DNA itself.

Chromosomes organize the sequence

Most human cells have two copies of each of chromosomes 1–22, one from each biological parent. These are the autosomes. X and Y are additional chromosomes; XX and XY are common combinations, but other complements and mosaic mixtures occur.

There is also a small circular genome inside mitochondria: mtDNA. It is usually inherited through the egg, and each cell can have many copies. The familiar “3.1 billion bases” describes roughly one set of nuclear chromosomes, not every DNA molecule in a cell.

A whole-genome test aims to sample across the genome. “Whole” does not guarantee that every position was read or every possible kind of change was detected.

Genes are only part of the genome

A gene is DNA used to make a functional RNA molecule. Many RNAs carry instructions to build a protein. Proteins do much of the work in cells; other DNA helps control where and when genes are used.

Only about 1–2% of human DNA directly codes for proteins. A change outside that fraction is not automatically irrelevant, and a change inside a gene is not automatically harmful.

Different from a reference does not mean wrong

A variant is a sequence difference relative to a chosen reference genome. For example, the reference may have A where one of your chromosome copies has G. Those versions are called alleles.

A reference is a shared map, not an ideal person. Most variants are harmless. To describe a change unambiguously, you need its chromosome, coordinate, reference and alternative letters, and the reference build.
