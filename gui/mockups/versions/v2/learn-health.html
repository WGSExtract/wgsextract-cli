Interpreting health findings

How software cross-references variants with clinical and drug-gene databases, and why medical hits belong with a clinician.

Before acting on anything health-related

WGS Extract runs research tools on personal files and cannot diagnose or rule out medical conditions. Never alter medication or screening from a screen here; bring concerning hits to a physician or genetic counsellor who can arrange accredited clinical testing.

How annotation labels a raw variant

Annotation tools like Ensembl’s Variant Effect Predictor (VEP) map each VCF (Variant Call Format) DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) change onto gene transcripts to predict RNA (ribonucleic acid) or protein effects alongside frequencies and clinical records. A missense swap substitutes one amino acid in a protein; many are benign, and labels shift across alternate transcripts.

Reading ClinVar entries and review stars

ClinVar (Clinical Variation database) gathers lab interpretations scored zero to four stars by reviewer consensus. Check the disorder, stars, dates and inheritance together: one recessive copy (0/1) ordinarily marks unaffected carrier status, and many dominant variants have incomplete penetrance.

Sparse or divided evidence earns a variant of uncertain significance (VUS) label, which guidelines treat as unactionable alone. Reports also cite the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) in two ways: the ACMG Secondary Findings list of actionable genes, and the joint ACMG and Association for Molecular Pathology (AMP) evidence rules labs use to classify variants.

Checking how common a variant is in gnomAD

The Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD) reports allele frequency (AF) across hundreds of thousands of people, counting chromosome copies (AF 0.01 = 1% of measured copies). High frequency rules out a solo cause of a rare Mendelian disorder, whereas absence from gnomAD is ordinary for benign private variants or under-sampled ancestries.

Computational scores rank candidates

  • Combined Annotation Dependent Depletion (CADD) ranks genome-wide changes: scaled 20 sits in the top 1% of substitutions, ranking candidates rather than stating disease odds.
  • Rare Exome Variant Ensemble Learner (REVEL) scores missense changes from 0 to 1, alongside structure models like AlphaMissense.
  • SpliceAI (splice-site artificial intelligence) scores from 0 to 1 whether a variant alters RNA exon splicing.

Because predictors share conservation inputs, matching high scores often reflect one shared signal and routinely flag benign variants.

Genes that influence how medicines work

Pharmacogenomics (PGx) studies genes affecting drug metabolism; the Pharmacogenomics Clinical Annotation Tool (PharmCAT) links genotypes to Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) guidance using named star alleles like CYP2C19*2.

Unread sites in a variant-only file like Helen’s VCF leave star-allele blind spots, and complex genes such as CYP2D6 (cytochrome P450 2D6) or human leukocyte antigen (HLA) loci require specialized callers and clinical typing. Review any PGx result with a physician or pharmacist alongside organ function and interacting drugs.

Polygenic scores and population percentiles

A polygenic score (PGS), or polygenic risk score (PRS) for disease, sums tiny weights across thousands of common alleles. Landing in the 80th percentile means scoring above 80% of a comparison group rather than having an 80% chance of illness; real risk depends on baseline prevalence, clinical factors and ancestry match.
