Variants & confidence

First ask whether a call is well supported. Then ask what it means.

Changes come in different shapes

  • SNV, single-nucleotide variant: one base is substituted. A SNP is a single-nucleotide polymorphism, a variable base in a population; people often use the terms loosely.
  • Indel, insertion or deletion: a few bases added or removed, usually under about 50 bases.
  • SV, structural variant: a larger deletion, duplication, reversal or rearrangement, conventionally around 50 bases or more.
  • CNV, copy-number variant: a segment has more or fewer copies than expected.
  • STR, short tandem repeat: a repeated motif whose count can vary. Very long repeats may require specialist testing.

One caller does not detect all of these reliably. An rsID is a database identifier, not a medical verdict; many variants have no rsID.

Read a genotype without guessing

A genotype describes the alleles called at a site. In a VCF, 0 means the reference allele, 1 the first alternative allele, 2 the second alternative if listed. For a two-copy site with reference A and alternative G:

  • 0/0 = A/A, two reference alleles.
  • 0/1 = A/G, one of each: heterozygous.
  • 1/1 = G/G, two alternative alleles: homozygous.
  • ./. = no complete genotype call, not A/A.

Zygosity describes whether copies match. Ploidy is how many copies exist in that region. A one-copy region may have a genotype such as 1, not 1/1. X/Y shared regions need different handling from their non-shared parts.

“Not in my VCF” does not mean “matches reference”

Suppose a marker at position 100 is absent from a variant-only VCF. Perhaps the reads confidently showed A/A. Or perhaps there were no usable reads, or a call was removed by filtering. The absence alone cannot distinguish these.

A gVCF or suitable read-based reference evidence can help, within the regions and quality limits actually assessed. Filling every absent marker with reference would turn gaps into invented certainty.

Confidence in the call is not confidence about disease

Read depth, base and mapping quality, genotype quality, allele balance and difficult-region flags help judge sequencing evidence. A caller’s PASS only means its configured filters were passed.

High-quality calls can be harmless; a medically important database label can sit on a sequencing artifact. ClinVar review status describes submitted interpretation evidence, not the quality of your reads.

Inspect unexpected findings in a genome browser and seek clinical confirmation before health decisions. A plausible de novo call in a child can still be an error or reflect a low-level parental variant.

Measured, phased and imputed are different

Phasing groups alleles onto chromosome copies. Two changes in one gene on the same copy differ from changes on opposite copies. The symbol | means phased within a phase set, but does not itself tell you which parent supplied each allele.

Imputation estimates unmeasured alleles from population patterns. Useful for some research, it is not replacement sequencing. Rare variants and poorly represented ancestries can be especially uncertain; do not treat imputed health findings as direct observations.
