Variants & confidence

How VCF files write down DNA differences, why missing rows stay uncertain and how to check read support behind an eye-catching call.

From single-letter swaps to large rearrangements

  • Single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) swap one DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) base for another (4.1 million in Helen’s VCF); common population SNVs are called single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs).
  • Insertions and deletions (indels) add or remove short stretches under ~50 bases (~800,000 in Helen’s file).
  • Structural variants (SVs) rearrange 50+ bases via deletions, duplications, inversions or translocations.
  • Copy-number variants (CNVs) delete or duplicate whole segments, altering copy count.
  • Short tandem repeats (STRs) repeat motifs like CAG; long expansions outgrow short reads and need specialist callers or lab assays.

Catalogued sites have a Reference SNP cluster identifier (rsID) such as rs429358 from dbSNP (Single Nucleotide Polymorphism Database). An rsID only means the position has been catalogued: millions of harmless common variants have one, and rare personal changes often do not.

Reading 0/0, 0/1 and 1/1 genotypes

A genotype codes the reference allele as 0 and alternate alleles as 1, 2 and so on. With reference A and alternate G on an autosome:

  • 0/0 = A/A, homozygous reference.
  • 0/1 = A/G, heterozygous (one of each).
  • 1/1 = G/G, homozygous alternate.
  • ./. = uncalled due to missing or weak reads.

Zygosity states whether two copies match or differ; ploidy counts chromosome copies at that locus. Outside the shared X/Y pseudoautosomal tips, Reginald has one X and one Y, so calls there are haploid (written 1, not 1/1).

Why an unlisted site in a plain VCF stays unknown

If position 100 is absent from Helen’s variant-only VCF, she might match the reference (0/0), have zero read coverage at that base or have had a noisy call filtered out. Without her BAM (Binary Alignment Map), CRAM (Compressed Reference-oriented Alignment Map) or a reference-block gVCF (Genomic VCF), nothing in her 148 MB file tells a clean reference call apart from a blind spot.

Checking the reads behind a call

A PASS flag only means routine caller thresholds were met. Before trusting a surprising call, check its depth, mapping quality, genotype quality (GQ) and allele balance: genuine autosomal 0/1 calls usually split reads near 50/50, not 2 out of 35.

Reads from repetitive DNA can misalign onto coordinates carrying serious ClinVar (Clinical Variation database) entries, because ClinVar stars rate the published literature on a variant, not whether your reads landed in the right place. Similarly, an apparent de novo variant in Reginald that neither Gordon nor Helen shows is most often a missed parent call or repeat artifact, with rare parental mosaicism as another possibility. Research files are not clinical diagnoses, so confirm any medically concerning call with a clinician or genetic counsellor.

The Genome viewer lays the reads out under the reference so you can check these by eye. Try a change seen only on one strand, reads that could map to two places and a real new variant to see how they differ.

Phasing and imputation

Phasing links variants onto individual parental chromosomes, writing 0|1 inside phase blocks built from long reads or trio comparisons across Gordon, Helen and Reginald.

Imputation estimates untyped common markers on microarrays or low-pass runs from population reference panels. Because those genotypes are inferred statistically from shared haplotypes, imputation misses rare personal variants and can guess wrong at individual sites.
