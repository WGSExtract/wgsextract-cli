Variants & confidence

How VCF files write down DNA differences, why missing rows stay uncertain and how to check read support behind an eye-catching call.

From single-letter swaps to large rearrangements

  • Single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) swap one DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) base for another (4.1 million in Helen’s Variant Call Format / VCF file); common population SNVs are called single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs).
  • Insertions and deletions (indels) add or remove short stretches under ~50 bases (~800,000 in Helen’s file).
  • Structural variants (SVs) rearrange 50+ bases via deletions, duplications, inversions or translocations.
  • Copy-number variants (CNVs) delete or duplicate whole segments, altering copy count.
  • Short tandem repeats (STRs) repeat motifs like CAG; long expansions outgrow short reads and need specialist callers or lab assays.

Catalogued sites carry a Reference SNP cluster identifier (rsID) like rs429358 from dbSNP (Single Nucleotide Polymorphism Database), a database tag carried by millions of benign variants while rare personal changes often have none.

Reading 0/0, 0/1 and 1/1 genotypes

A genotype codes the reference allele as 0 and alternate alleles as 1, 2 and so on. With reference A and alternate G on an autosome:

  • 0/0 = A/A, homozygous reference.
  • 0/1 = A/G, heterozygous (one of each).
  • 1/1 = G/G, homozygous alternate.
  • ./. = uncalled due to missing or weak reads.

Zygosity states whether two copies match or differ; ploidy counts chromosome copies at that locus. Outside the shared X/Y pseudoautosomal tips, Reginald has one X and one Y, so calls there are haploid (1 rather than 1/1).

Why an unlisted site in a plain VCF stays unknown

If position 100 is absent from Helen’s variant-only VCF, she might be 0/0, or depth there dropped to zero, or a noisy call failed filtering. Without her BAM (Binary Alignment Map), CRAM (Compressed Reference-oriented Alignment Map) or a reference-block gVCF (Genomic VCF), nothing in her 148 MB file tells a clean reference call apart from a blind spot.

Checking read support before trusting a headline label

Check depth, mapping quality, genotype quality (GQ) and allele balance (true autosomal 0/1 calls usually split reads near 50/50 rather than 2 of 35); PASS only means routine caller thresholds were met.

Misaligned repeat reads can land on coordinates carrying serious ClinVar (Clinical Variation database) labels, since ClinVar stars grade published literature rather than your read pileup. Likewise, an apparent de novo call in Reginald absent from Gordon and Helen is usually a missed parent call or repeat artifact (or rare parental mosaicism), so inspect the reads and confirm clinically before acting.

Phased chromosome copies versus imputed guesses

Phasing links variants onto individual parental chromosomes, writing 0|1 inside phase blocks built from long reads or trio comparisons across Gordon, Helen and Reginald.

Imputation infers untyped common markers on microarrays or low-pass runs from population panels; it misses rare variants and should never replace direct sequencing for medical questions.
